# Changelog

## 0.37.0
**From a site error to a closed ticket, automatically.** When your website or app reports an error (the new `app.error` event, or a webhook from your site), the agent opens a Jira bug (or adds to the matching one), writes a dated row on your Confluence known-issues page, and tells Slack and Teams who hit it, which page and what they clicked. When the team fixes it (a `bug.resolved` event, `/fixed KAN-12 note` in Slack, or the ticket being closed in Jira) the agent adds the fix note, closes the ticket, marks the Confluence row Fixed with the date, and tells the team.
**Bugs reported by text, call or email** become tickets too: text keywords, a caller who reports a problem (new AI action `report_bug`), and an optional email workflow (off until you switch it on).
**Your site can post errors straight to the agent.** A custom webhook connection can now list your website address so the browser may send the error from the page (cross-origin allowed only for the addresses you list).
**Per-bundle kits:** 40 bundles now get their own workflows, phone welcome and common call-answering lines.
**New ready-made workflows:** site down, order failed, checkout abandoned, bad review, payment dispute, low stock.
**Fixed.** Booking a table twice with the same name and time re-confirms instead of double-booking; the knowledge search finds rare words; a call that connects to a person no longer says nobody could pick up; calendar bookings are titled "Reservation: table for N" and carry the phone number so reminders work.
New: a guide and helper for connecting Google Calendar with write access (`docs/google-calendar.md`, `tools/connect_google_calendar.py`).
Checked for this release: all 641 tests pass, plus a real run on a demo restaurant: website 403 error, Jira ticket, Slack and Teams alerts, then closed on fix. Not yet tested with independent customers.

## 0.36.0
**A ready-made set of workflows for your kind of business.** Every industry now has its own complete kit, written in plain words and built from what that business really does with customers: bookings and reminders, "your order is ready", refills, intake, forms, missed visits, estimates, vehicle ready, waitlists, check-in and checkout, missed calls, after hours, and a person for anything sensitive. Clinics, pharmacies, dispensaries, law firms, finance, collections, prisons, public services and schools get safe-by-design wording: no promotion, no diagnoses or medical or legal advice, no personal details in a text, a person for anything sensitive, and honest limits. This is not a claim of HIPAA or any other certification; you stay responsible for your own rules and agreements.
**You approve the wording first.** A kit workflow starts switched off and says "Needs your OK". Open it, read it in plain words, change the wording or when it runs, and press **Approve and turn on**. Nothing is sent to a customer before that, and after that it still follows consent, STOP, quiet hours, limits and Practice mode. Your changes are kept in your own agent folder, so a bundle update never overwrites them. In a health, legal, finance or public business the wording you type is checked, and a promotion or a health detail is refused.
**The workflow list is easier to use.** It shows ten at a time and the rest load as you scroll inside the same panel, with Needs your OK, On and Off filters. Every workflow and description is now in everyday words ("Reminds customers before their booking", "Tells customers their order is ready"), on the dashboard, in the guides and in the README.
**A smart reminder that does what you choose.** One reminder workflow: tick the day before, the morning of, two hours before, or others, and it says the line that fits each one (a reminder the day before, a good-morning line on the day, a "coming up soon" line in the last hours). It never reminds after a booking has started, it skips the morning line when the booking is too early for it, and it only looks at calendar entries whose title contains the word you choose (Appointment by default). It replaces the fixed day-before and two-hours pair.
**Answers while you are closed.** Set your opening days and times once and the "while you are closed" workflow replies to texts sent outside them. Until you set them it stays quiet (unknown is never treated as closed). Health, public and home-service versions name the emergency number.
**Announce a sale (shops, restaurants, salons, laundries and car businesses only).** List the pages (and, if you have one, a product list) to watch. When a new sale appears the agent writes a short text from the words on that page and shows it to you as a draft. You can change it, preview exactly what customers receive and who is left out, send yourself a test, and only then press Approve. It goes only to people on your consent list, each with how and when they agreed; anyone who replies STOP leaves for good and only a new DEALS from them brings them back; it is sent only inside your sending hours (9 a.m. to 8 p.m. by default), in small batches within your hourly limit, and at most a few sale texts per person per month. In Practice mode the whole run is rehearsed and nothing is sent. It is never offered to health, pharmacy, dispensary, legal, finance, public-service, school, prison or collections businesses. Honest note: sale texts generally need the person's prior written agreement (in the US, the TCPA, state laws and carrier rules); this is a tool, not legal advice, and Speakvora cannot check that your list is real.
**Every bundle has its own kit.** On top of its industry family's kit, each of the 40 bundles (restaurants, bars and food trucks, healthcare, telehealth, pharmacy, dispensary, hotels, airport parking and taxi, retail, furniture, fashion and hobby stores, marketplace sellers, dropshipping, returns, beauty and fitness, studios and coworking, laundry, home services, outdoor trades, automotive, car care and roadside, schools, tutoring, government and utilities, legal, lending, billing, pawn and rent-to-own, customer service, phone menus, dating and social apps, IT support, hosting, apps and devices, everyday, wayfinding, kiosks, warehouse, jails) ships 3 to 6 workflows only that business type needs, a spoken welcome for its phone line, and 12 common call-answering lines (take a message, one moment, callback, repeat please, directions...). The welcome plays before the phone menu when your pack has it. See `docs/workflows-by-bundle.md`.
**New phrases.** The kits add spoken lines per industry; they are listed per industry in `voice/phrases/workflow-needs/` and are generated with the usual phrase pipeline. Until a pack has a line, English packs say the plain built-in sentence and the missing line is logged.
**Fixed.** A line missing from an English pack used by a Slack /say command now falls back to the plain sentence too; a text blocked only by the hourly limit is retried later instead of being dropped (found by the new tests).
Checked for this release: 579 earlier tests plus the new kit, editing, reminder, after-hours and sale tests (see docs/ for what each covers). Not yet tested with independent customers or a physical phone line, and the new phrases are not yet generated or voiced.

## 0.35.4
**A new “Your agent” screen.** The Automations tab is now Your agent: four boxes in one clear screen. Agent preview (try a text, a call or an app event, and see what your agent knows), Automations (Enabled, Needs setup, All), Phone menu and Bookings. Every box shows in full; the page scrolls, on a computer and on a phone.
**One switch for Live and Practice.** It sits at the top of every tab, shows your status, and switching to Live still runs the go-live checks first. The old banners and “Go live” rows are gone.
**Settings in four boxes** (Ask before sending, AI controls, Your business, Your account) and the bar at the bottom now stays at the bottom edge.
**Open your dashboard on your own devices.** With Tailscale, one button (Connect, Permanent address, Open my dashboard on my devices) makes your dashboard open at `https://your-name.ts.net:8443/dashboard` on your phone or laptop, only when signed in to your own Tailscale, and still with your access token. The public address never shows the dashboard. Eight wrong tokens in ten minutes lock the sender out.
**A person, fast.** A caller who says support, customer service, technical support, a manager, a department, “a person” (or the same in Spanish, French, German, Portuguese or Italian) hears “Ok, I'll be connecting you to our support department now” and is connected to your staff phone, or to the matching department on your phone menu, at once. A caller who sounds frustrated (about 290 phrases: anger, swearing, “you're not listening”, “this is a joke”, “I want a refund”, waiting too long, the same sentence twice) hears “I'm sorry about that” and is connected too. It works without your AI, and you can add your own phrases or switch it off (Settings, AI controls, Built-in safeguards).
**Help for customers who are deaf, hard of hearing, or find speaking hard.** Built for the people who call and text your business. A stutter (“b-b-book a t-t-table, um, f-for four”) is understood as “book a table for four”, and your Calls list keeps both what was heard and what it was understood as (when you keep transcripts). A caller who stops mid-sentence hears “Take your time, I'm listening” and has up to 12 seconds to finish; the parts are joined before the agent answers. A caller who is hard of hearing (“speak up”, “I can't hear you”) gets slower, louder speech for the rest of the call and an offer of a text. A caller who is deaf or asks for a text is texted straight away and the conversation continues in writing; press star does the same. How your business handles it depends on its type: clinics, pharmacies, legal, finance and public services put a person first and tell your team; shops, restaurants and trades offer a text first; app support lines offer a text and tell your team. When your AI is connected it checks your business and chooses; you can change it any time (Settings, AI controls). Not yet tested with live relay (TTY) services or with a real caller on a phone line.
**Fixed.** Times your AI suggests are written a.m./p.m. so the voice says them in full; recorded lines come first in a call's shortlist; recording a phrase no longer shows a false error.
